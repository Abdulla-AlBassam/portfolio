I have kept notes for years without ever showing them to anyone. Most of them are half a page long, written in the twenty minutes after something went wrong, and they were never meant to be read twice. Going back through them recently, I noticed that the useful ones all had the same shape: a thing I believed, a thing that turned out to be true instead, and the gap between the two. That gap is the only part worth publishing.

So this section exists for the pieces that survive being read a second time. It is not a changelog, and it is not a place to announce what I am working on. The projects page already does that, and it does it better, because a project can be linked to and run. Writing is for the parts of the work that do not fit in a repository: the reasoning, the false starts, and the occasional conclusion that only became obvious once it was too late to act on it.

## What I expect to write about

Mostly security, because that is where I spend my days and where the gap between belief and reality is widest. Some of it will be about building things, which is a slower and more forgiving discipline. A little of it will be about photography, which taught me more about paying attention than any tool ever has.

![A framed drawing hanging in a gallery, photographed on film](/thoughts/hello/desk.webp "A drawing in a gallery in Barcelona. I spent longer looking at the frame than at the drawing, which is a habit I am trying to break.")

I would rather write four pieces a year that are worth someone's time than one a month that is not. That is a low bar to set publicly and a difficult one to hold, so I am writing it down here where it is awkward to ignore. If a piece cannot survive the question of what it is actually claiming, it does not go up.

> The point of writing something down is not to remember it. It is to find out whether you understood it in the first place.

There is a quieter reason too. Explaining a thing in plain language is the fastest way I know to discover that I only understood the shape of it. Every draft so far has collapsed at least one paragraph that felt solid in my head, and rebuilding that paragraph has usually changed my mind about something. If nobody reads a single one of these, the drafts will still have earned their keep.

Either way, the first real piece is already in progress, and it starts with a mistake.
